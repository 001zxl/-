# Pattern Library

Append concise learnings here after successful ecommerce image work.

## Initial Patterns

### Product-Preserved Viral Sticker

Category/platform: snack / Douyin ecommerce.

Observed pattern: real product in the center, bright geometric background, thick black headline, short sticker labels.

Why it works: preserves trust while adding short-video energy.

Prompt tactic: begin with "use the reference product as the real main product; preserve packaging, logo, transparent material, and product texture" before describing the bold style.

Risk: generated text may be wrong; keep labels short and large.

### Clean Trust Detail

Category/platform: packaged food / marketplace detail page.

Observed pattern: product centered on white or light gray background, with thin-line callouts and lots of negative space.

Why it works: increases product credibility and reduces visual fatigue after loud hero images.

Prompt tactic: ask for "minimal premium white-background product photography" and "large clean callouts only".

Risk: can become too plain; keep texture, shadows, and product scale strong.

### Macro Proof

Category/platform: food / detail page proof module.

Observed pattern: macro crop of the edible product or package window, with magnifier/callout and one proof headline.

Why it works: converts vague quality claims into visual evidence.

Prompt tactic: define foreground macro subject separately from background packaging.

Risk: model may generate unrealistic product pieces; keep original product photos as references.

### Process Card

Category/platform: traditional food / trust module.

Observed pattern: numbered process cards beside product and process props.

Why it works: creates craftsmanship credibility without overloading the main image.

Prompt tactic: limit to 3-4 process steps and keep words short.

Risk: process claims must be verified.

### Variant Shelf

Category/platform: multi-flavor snack / carousel.

Observed pattern: flavor/color variants in the back row, hero SKU in the front.

Why it works: communicates choice while still steering attention to the primary SKU.

Prompt tactic: name "background variants" and "foreground hero product" explicitly.

Risk: model may invent flavors; provide variant reference images.

### Beauty Texture Swatch

Category/platform: skincare, cosmetics / detail page.

Observed pattern: product packshot plus cream/liquid/powder texture swatch, ingredient mood props, clean labels.

Why it works: makes abstract skin-feel or color payoff visible.

Prompt tactic: preserve packaging exactly, then render texture separately as a macro foreground element.

Risk: before-after or efficacy claims need user-provided proof.

### Apparel Fit And Fabric

Category/platform: clothing, shoes, accessories / carousel and detail page.

Observed pattern: model or mannequin fit shot plus fabric/stitching close-up and outfit pairing.

Why it works: reduces uncertainty around size, drape, material, and styling.

Prompt tactic: separate "fit context" from "fabric proof"; preserve color accuracy.

Risk: generated bodies, sizes, or colors can mislead; request simple poses and verify SKU color.

### Electronics Feature Callout

Category/platform: electronics, accessories / marketplace detail page.

Observed pattern: clean product render/photo, exploded/callout lines, ports or interface close-ups, scenario desk/car/home setup.

Why it works: turns specs into visible utility.

Prompt tactic: keep text short, show one feature per module, and avoid inventing certifications or specs.

Risk: model may hallucinate ports, screens, and logos; use reference photos for the exact device.

### Home Scale Scene

Category/platform: home goods, storage, decor / detail page.

Observed pattern: product in a real room with human-scale or furniture-scale cues, plus material close-up.

Why it works: helps shoppers understand size, placement, and daily use.

Prompt tactic: define room style, product scale, and one material proof area.

Risk: room scene can bury the product; keep product dominant and crop mobile-first.

### Problem-Solution Split

Category/platform: cross-category / conversion module.

Observed pattern: left side problem state, right side product-assisted improved state, with one concise benefit.

Why it works: quickly communicates why the product matters.

Prompt tactic: avoid exaggerated before-after; keep both sides realistic and compliant.

Risk: medical, beauty, and safety categories require careful claims.

### Bundle Value Grid

Category/platform: consumables, accessories, home goods / promo carousel.

Observed pattern: hero product plus included items or variants arranged in a clean grid with simple quantity labels.

Why it works: communicates value and completeness without fake discounts.

Prompt tactic: only show included items that the user provides or confirms.

Risk: do not invent bundle contents.

## 2026-08 Snack Batch Learnings

### UGC Real-Use Upgrade

Category/platform: snack / Douyin ecommerce detail page.

Observed pattern: real product pouch in a casual living-room or desk scene, with one human action cue such as holding or opening the package, plus clean ecommerce labels.

Why it works: bridges social proof and product detail; feels less templated than pure studio wood-table scenes.

Prompt tactic: ask for "phone-shot feeling but professionally cleaned" and explicitly forbid clutter, faces, monitors, and platform logos.

Risk: hands and packaging seals can distort; inspect opening area and keep the product front readable.

### Comic Pop Snack Cover

Category/platform: snack / livestream cover, promo carousel, short-video hook.

Observed pattern: photo-real product centered inside a red/yellow/black comic burst with halftone dots, motion seeds, and short sound-effect headline.

Why it works: creates a high-energy test variant when the batch feels visually fatigued.

Prompt tactic: keep the first sentence focused on product preservation, then describe the pop-art layer as background and motion graphics around the product.

Risk: can become too loud for strict marketplace main images; use as campaign or A/B creative unless the platform accepts it.

### Surreal Product World

Category/platform: snack, beverage, beauty, home fragrance / detail page memory point.

Observed pattern: oversized hero product on a clean stage with floating ingredients, light trails, premium props, and a simple benefit headline.

Why it works: adds novelty while still letting the product own the frame.

Prompt tactic: define "premium, not cartoon" and keep props abstract or secondary so the model does not bury the SKU.

Risk: can hallucinate decorative claims or make the scene feel fake; preserve packaging from the strongest front reference and keep text draft-only.
